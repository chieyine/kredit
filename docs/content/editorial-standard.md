# Kredit editorial standard

Write for someone making a sale, checking a payment or resolving a specific problem. Answer their question early. Use the amount, date, document or next action that makes the answer useful.

- Give each guide its own argument and examples. Do not expand a topic seed with shared paragraphs, interchangeable FAQs or keyword suffixes.
- Stop when the question is answered. Article counts, word counts and section counts are not quality targets.
- Mark invented examples as examples. Do not manufacture customer stories, quotations, research, testimonials or publication history.
- Describe what Kredit actually does. Check fee triggers, access permissions and collection conditions against the implementation. Avoid absolute promises about safety, privacy or repayment.
- Include sources where they support a specific factual claim. Explain their relevance. Do not add official links merely to make a guide look researched.
- Record publication dates only when publication occurs. Update dates when the content is materially edited.
- Read the finished page aloud. Replace vague reassurance, forced simplicity and repetitive slogans with concrete information. Automated checks support this review; they cannot replace it.

A topic remains in the unpublished backlog until its own copy and page have been reviewed.
